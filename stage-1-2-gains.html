Remap Stages FAQ

What is the difference between stage 1 and stage 2 remap?

Stage 1 is an ECU-only software remap with no hardware changes needed. It works on a completely stock vehicle and typically adds 20-30% more power. Stage 2 requires supporting modifications — usually a decat/sports exhaust and upgraded intercooler — and delivers 30-50% more power. The ECU map is specifically written for the hardware fitted.

How much BHP does a stage 1 remap add?

It depends on the vehicle and engine. Turbocharged diesels typically see the biggest gains — a 2.0 TDI might gain 40-60 BHP. Turbocharged petrol engines often gain 30-50 BHP. Naturally aspirated engines see smaller gains of 5-15%.

Is a stage 2 remap worth it?

If you've already fitted a sports exhaust, decat, or intercooler upgrade, stage 2 unlocks the full potential of that hardware. Without the supporting mods, stage 1 is a better option. Stage 2 typically costs more due to the hardware investment but delivers significantly more power.

Will a stage 1 remap damage my engine?

A professional stage 1 remap stays within the safe operating limits of your engine, turbo, and drivetrain. OEM components are engineered with safety margins. However, it's critical to use a reputable tuner, quality fuel, and keep up with maintenance.

Can I go straight to stage 2?

You can, but you need the supporting hardware fitted first. The tuner will need to know exactly what modifications are on the vehicle to write a safe and optimised stage 2 map. Without the right hardware, a stage 2 map could cause issues.

Does remapping affect insurance?

Yes, ECU remapping is a modification and should be declared to your insurance company. Some specialist insurers offer competitive policies for modified vehicles. Failure to declare can void your cover.
